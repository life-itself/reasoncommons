---

I think we're assuming everyone has to move through the same progression. First contact, then practice, then contributing, then stewarding. But the communities I work with wouldn't recognise that shape at all — some of them start with the doing and never get near the discourse. A single path is going to lose them at step one.

---

it's in the small tents that the actual work happens. big gatherings are good for morale and terrible for anything else. every real change I've seen came out of five people who kept showing up.

---

Honestly I don't think "throughput" can be defined here without becoming a metric someone games. The moment you count adoption events you get people producing adoption events. I know the doc says it won't reduce to activity counts but I've never seen that hold once there's a number.

---

The maps being versioned and challengeable is good but that on its own doesn't make the group learn anything. You can have a beautifully maintained wiki that nobody's mind is ever changed by. Learning happens when someone is actually made to answer an objection, and that's a social practice, not a document format.

---

Facilitator development isn't just about replication — the people who learn to facilitate are the ones who end up noticing what isn't working and saying so. That's how the group learns. Feels like it should point at both.

---

None of this addresses money. Who is paying for any of it. Every group I've been part of that ignored this ended up quietly run by whoever happened to have savings, and nobody voted for that.

---

We keep talking as if the problem is that people don't convert. In my experience they convert fine and then leave after eight months because nothing changed for them personally. The leak is at the back, not the front.
