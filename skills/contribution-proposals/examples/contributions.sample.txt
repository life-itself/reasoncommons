Marta Oyelaran

I run a permaculture cooperative outside Coimbra, eleven of us, going on six years now. I'll be honest, I came to this because a friend sent it to me and I'm not sure I follow all the language in your documents. But here is what I know. We spent the first two years talking about values. Reading, discussing, going on retreats. Nothing changed. What changed us was the third year, when we took on four hectares of degraded land and had to actually feed ourselves from it. You learn cooperation very fast when the irrigation fails in August. People who could not agree in a meeting agreed in a field. So I think something is being missed here, which is that most people do not change their views and then change their lives. They change their lives, usually because they have to, and the views follow after. If you want the durable thing you keep talking about, I suspect you need people doing something together that has real consequences if they get it wrong.

Kwame Boateng

Adding to what Marta said, because I think it's the same point from a different angle. I trained as a carpenter. Nobody learns carpentry from a book and nobody learns it alone. You stand next to someone for two years and you ruin a lot of wood. Whatever this group is trying to pass on, if there isn't an apprenticeship shape to it — someone experienced, someone new, real work between them, and time — it will not pass on.

Ana Beatriz Lima

I want to talk about money, but not in the way people usually mean it here. I've spent nine years working on mutual credit systems in Belo Horizonte, and the thing I keep seeing is that the currency people use decides what they are able to do with each other. If you need euros to take part, then taking part is rationed by who has euros, and that isn't a values problem, it's a plumbing problem. Everyone in our network can trade hours without having to earn first, and it changed who showed up — completely. What I notice in your model is that it assumes people can give time to practice and to stewarding. But the people who most need what you're describing are exactly the ones whose hours are already sold. I don't know where this goes in your diagram. I just don't think you can leave it out.
