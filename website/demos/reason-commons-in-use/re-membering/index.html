The tension behind the choices

Enough safety and enough openness

Protection from overwhelm and the ability to adapt are both legitimate needs. The proposed conflict asks whether sufficient safety really requires imposed individual regulation. Participatory safeguards are a possible response to that assumption.

What could change

Support, choice and learning together

Offer practices people can adapt or leave; make one tool contestable and reversible; act, listen and retune. These routes contribute jointly to a lived, revisable practice. Pressure to appear calm and surveillance remain possible harms; safeguards need to work in practice.

What must become possible

Readiness belongs to a willing group

A host must agree the situation and criteria, make consent and withdrawal usable, secure time and repair arrangements, and establish actual control over the tool. These are proposed readiness conditions, not completed preparations.

How the proposal could be tried

Map, agree, try, listen and retune

An illustrative sequence maps the setting and agrees a bounded purpose. A voluntary practice and a tool review can then proceed in parallel. Participants assess what helped, harmed or surprised them and revise or stop. No host, trial or owner is assigned.

The argument behind the practice

Body, relationships and a wider world

Heath’s talk moves through embodied cognition, predictive processing, extended minds and shared timing toward the somatic commons. Evidence for coupling, contested constitution of mind and philosophical participation remain distinct. These six views are temporary analytical cuts through reciprocal processes across timescales; they are the first bounded application, not eighteen completed maps of Body, We and World.
