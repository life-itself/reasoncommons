Body, tools and a wider world

The talks move from Bateson’s walking stick and embodied cognition through predictive processing, extended minds, shared timing and repair, to tools and environments that shape us in return. This commons selects the practical argument about tending those conditions. It is not a complete map of every thinker or argument in the talks.

Evidence and philosophical commitments

The script distinguishes evidence for coupling from the contested claim that a tool constitutes part of a mind, and from the author’s philosophical starting point in participation and process. The coupling–constitution objection and predictive-processing boundary debate remain open. The claim that all regulation is co-regulation is the author’s synthesis, not a result established here. The practical goal does not require agreement with that ontology.

Which document takes precedence?

The spoken script with notes supplies the main qualifications: consent, freedom to step out, harm and repair, and the difference between cited work and Heath’s own extensions. The slides give the compact sequence; the extended passages retain longer versions and material parked for later talks. They are companion versions, not three independent bodies of evidence. Cultural concepts are situated in living traditions, not treated as interchangeable names for one idea.

What counts as progress?

No actual group, baseline, trial owner or outcome is established. Before trying a practice, participants would need to agree how to judge benefit, pressure, safety and agency, including whether declining is possible in practice. Quietness or compliance alone would not settle those questions. Conflicts, prerequisites and assigned actions remain unestablished.
