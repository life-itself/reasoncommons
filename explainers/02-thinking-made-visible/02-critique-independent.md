# Independent critique — script v1

**Verdict:** competent draft with one structural problem (the eight-list drags; the opening
recycles Part 1's closing device) and one logical problem (the central claim is asserted
with a false-sounding flourish rather than earned).

## The load-bearing defect

> "Nobody has ever, in the history of mind maps, said *I don't accept that arrow*."

This is the load-bearing sentence of the piece and **it is simply false**. People contest
flowchart logic constantly — *that shouldn't come before this*, *that decision node is
wrong*. What is actually true, and far more defensible, is that there is no *criterion* for
contesting an arrow — no rule a challenge has to satisfy, and therefore none it can win by.
The piece has the right idea and reaches for the falsifiable, false version of it.

"That is the entire difference between a diagram and an argument" then builds directly on
the shaky sentence, and overclaims in its own right.

## Other findings

- **The opening duplicates Part 1's ending.** Five confident, different answers — fired twice in a row across consecutive pieces. Reads as reused material rather than deliberate reprise.
- **The link to Part 1 is thin** until the final two lines. One early sentence tying "which one is right" to the guessing frame would fix it for both cold and warm readers.
- **Announcing the count** ("there are eight of them") primes the reader for a slog before a single test has been asked.
- **Test 3 is the weak link** — the only test with an instruction rather than a story, sitting between a crisp definitional test and the vivid oxygen one. Exactly where the list first sags. Cut or merge.
- **"None of them requires any expertise"** conflates *asking* the question with *answering* it. Only the first is expertise-free — knowing that fire needs oxygen is precisely domain knowledge.
- **The "they believe it" paragraph** is too tidy about how reliably externalising reasoning defuses ego and status.
- **Ends on a handoff, not an ending.** "One map that five people built and nobody could break" is the real close; five lines of Part 3 setup follow it.
- **Narrator intrusions:** "This one is worth slowing down for", "it is the whole trick", "The fix sounds almost too simple."
- **Mid-paragraph bolding** reads as content-marketing formatting rather than long-form prose.

## Protect in revision

"Nothing has changed except that it is now outside you, where it can be looked at." · "There
is a feeling with a conclusion attached." · "And nobody can see it. Not even the person
holding it." · "Every argument has an oxygen: the condition so ordinary that nobody names
it, right up until the day it isn't there." · "Anglers on the bank did not *cause* good
fishing. They are how you know the fishing is good." · "When the reasoning is out on the
page, the page takes the criticism." · "one map that five people built and nobody could
break — which is a different kind of object entirely."
