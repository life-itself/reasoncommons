# Draft — A2, *Squeeze before you spend*

Series A, chapter 2 of 8. Bead `reasoncommons-bnl.26`. Prose only, no visual direction. Follows [A1](../a1-sixty-people-waiting/script.md), which ends on Maria absorbing anger something else was generating.

Facts carried from the published piece: eight-hour day, six hours clinical, two on admin, twenty patients, twenty-six when the admin hours come back. A1 established capacities 60 / 45 / 20 / 50 / 40 and demand of sixty a day.

---

**Key claim:** Before paying to expand the constraint, get everything you can out of it — and expect it to move once you do.

---

So the clinic knows where its constraint is. It has also just been given enough money to hire one more person. The obvious move is to hire another doctor.

Not yet. Watch her day first.

Dr Adeyemi works eight hours. She sees patients for about six of them. The other two go on things that do not need a medical degree: re-taking a history the triage nurse already took, hunting for notes, printing and signing forms. She stays until seven most evenings writing up what she had no time to write in the room.

Twenty patients in six hours is a shade under eighteen minutes each. Give the two hours back at the same rate and the same doctor, in the same building, on the same day, sees twenty-six.

Six more people a day. Around fifteen hundred a year. The man with the cough is one of them. It costs nothing.

**Before you buy more of the constraint, stop wasting the constraint you already have.**

This is the step almost everybody skips, and it is the hiring decision made smaller: you are not adding capacity to the system, you are moving capacity toward the constraint. It just happened that the cheapest place to find some was already inside it.

It has an uncomfortable consequence. Everyone else now has to run at whatever pace keeps Dr Adeyemi working — which means reception deliberately slowing down, and every local number you have showing those people getting worse while the clinic gets better. If you cannot tolerate that on a dashboard, none of this is available to you.

Only after that do you spend the money, and you spend it on the doctor, not on the loudest queue.

And then the whole thing moves. Take the doctor past forty and follow-up booking, which can only handle forty, becomes the constraint. Fix that and it is pharmacy. Push far enough and every station inside the building outruns the number of people who come through the door, and the constraint has left the building altogether — it is now the hospital that will not take referrals any faster. Which is why the most expensive sentence anyone in that clinic can say is *we solved that one*.

So: find it, get everything out of it, make everything else serve it, only then expand it, and when it moves, start again.

One thing we have not asked. Those two hours of admin — the re-taken history, the hunt for notes, the forms — why are they there at all? Nobody designed Dr Adeyemi's day to include them. That question turns out to have an answer, and it is not the one anybody at Ashfield would guess.
