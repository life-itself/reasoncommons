# Script — B3, *Give every claim an address*

Series B, chapter 3 of 5. Bead `reasoncommons-bnl.18`. Revised against [`critique.md`](critique.md); the draft is in [`draft.md`](draft.md). Prose only, no visual direction. 588 words.

**Key claim:** A tree of claims gives every claim, and every link between them, a place you can point to — so Maren's suggestion has somewhere to land.

---

An identity you can say out loud, other claims pointing at it, and an amendment that carries to everything pointing. Reasoning can have all three, and the way to get them is older than the tooling.

Take what you are trying to achieve and write it as one claim. Then ask what has to be true for it to happen, and write each of those down. Then ask the same question of each of them, and keep going for as long as the answers are still telling you something.

The question is narrower than it sounds, and the narrowness is the whole method. Not what would help — what it fails without. *In order to have this, we need that.* In order for a clinic to see more patients, we need the doctor to have more hours. A second receptionist would help, so it goes on some other list; the clinic sees the same twenty patients with two receptionists as with one, so it is not a condition and it does not go in the tree.

What you end up with is a tree of claims, in which each link is a claim too: it asserts that the lower thing is necessary for the upper one. Links can be wrong on their own — the condition underneath can be true and still not be something the claim above needs. You do not have to be trained to argue with a tree — you argue with a box, or you argue with an arrow.

The method is not ours: it comes from the Logical Thinking Process, used on factories and hospitals for decades. How these trees get built, and how you check a link without taking anyone's word for it, is what the other series is about — see *What should be happening? The goal tree* and *An arrow is a claim you can check*.

Earlier this year we built one for the Second Renaissance, out of the papers rather than out of a workshop. At the root: a Second Renaissance that is more than an idea. Under it, eight conditions that all have to hold at once — among them *people come to understand the crises as one crisis*, and *small dense groups make the new way of living livable*.

That second one now has an address. It is a node you can name in a sentence to someone who was not in the room, everything that depends on it points at it, and if it is reworded tomorrow, everything pointing at it points at the new wording. This is what the forum and the papers could not give it.

Which is where Maren's sentence goes. She wrote:

> The theory of change seems to float free of any actual place. You should start in a neighbourhood somebody actually lives in.

Read in the tree's grammar, she is proposing a further condition under that node: *in order for small dense groups to make the new way of living livable, we need them rooted in a place somebody actually lives in.* A box, and an arrow, hanging under the condition they qualify. She is not saying the movement should stop caring about small dense groups.

**Her proposal has an address before anyone has agreed with it, and that is what makes disagreeing with her cheap.**

Somebody still has to decide that is what she meant. That reading is ours, not hers, and it could be wrong — which is an argument the tree can now hold, because there is a specific arrow to say no to.
