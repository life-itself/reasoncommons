# Script — B4, *Why nobody does the filing*

Series B, chapter 4 of 5. Bead `reasoncommons-bnl.20`. Revised against [`critique.md`](critique.md); the draft is in [`draft.md`](draft.md). Prose only, no visual direction. 597 words.

---

**Key claim:** Structure takes effort, so it looks like a choice between easy and cumulative — but that rests on one assumption: that the person contributing has to do the filing.

---

Notice who did the filing a moment ago. Maren wrote one sentence on a forum. Somebody then had to read it, find the condition it bears on, work out that it proposed a further condition underneath rather than an objection to the whole, and phrase it as *in order for small dense groups to make the new way of living livable, we need them rooted in a place somebody actually lives in*. That somebody was us, by hand, for one sentence.

Which is the same disease we diagnosed in the papers, wearing a better outfit. We have not removed the cost of filing. We have moved it.

Had Maren done it herself, it would have been a morning's work before she said anything — learn what a box and an arrow mean here, read enough of the tree to find which of the eight conditions her point touches, put it in the grammar. She has a dissertation to write, and she came to the forum because a conversation was happening.

This is not about newcomers being lazy. Nobody joined a movement for civilizational renewal in order to fill in forms, and the people already inside don't do it either: the weekly circle takes minutes, which is the cheap version, and even that produced nothing the movement holds. Effort is not the only reason — we also disagree about what the tree should say — but it is the reason that has just changed.

So it looks like a straight choice. Keep contributing easy, and accept that the good parts dissolve into the scroll. Or make it structured so the thinking compounds, and accept that most people will not pay the entry price. We want the easy version for its own sake, too — unstructured talk is where Maren's sentence came from in the first place, and nothing more careful would have produced it.

Both horns serve the same thing: a group whose thinking compounds. When two things we want look incompatible, it is usually worth hunting for the assumption underneath rather than picking a side. (That hunt has a name, the evaporating cloud, and a chapter of its own in the other series: *Why hasn't somebody fixed it? The cloud*.)

The assumption here is that the reading has to be done at the moment of contributing, by someone who already knows both the sentence and the structure — which in practice meant the contributor, or nobody. Machines could always file by topic, keyword and tag. What none of them could do was say what a sentence asserts, and against what. The assumption was never a law. It was a description of what used to be affordable.

**That assumption is no longer true in the age of AI.**

We have built the reader and run it: a batch of contributions, read against this same tree, each one proposed against a specific node with a confidence and, where it was unsure, a second reading — and one contribution it could not place at all. The next chapter is that run, in full. It is a prototype, and it is not good enough to leave alone.

It doesn't have to be. The machine proposes; people decide what goes in. Deciding on a proposal is a fraction of the work of producing one — though it is real work, and it lands on whoever maintains the tree, so the cost has moved again rather than vanished. What has changed is Maren's side of it. She writes her sentence the way she was always going to write it, and nothing is asked of her that was not asked before.

## Source note for the build

Effort is not the only reason groups don't work this way — people also disagree about what the tree should say, and the tooling has never been usable. The script hedges this in one clause; keep the hedge. A series whose companion chapter is about five people giving five different causes cannot afford an unhedged single-cause answer.

Real: the weekly research circle and its minutes (B1's source note governs). The AI run is real — Demo C, `talk/2r-research-group/demo-c/proposals.yaml`, read against `ltp/ltp-model.yaml`: nine contributions, each with a proposed placement, a confidence, alternative readings where the reading was uncertain, and one the tree could not hold. **The contributors in that batch are fictional and the demo says so; the reading is real.** B5 shows it properly, so B4 must stay at two sentences and not spend B5's payoff.

Composite: Maren, her sentence, and the five-step cost — a reconstruction of what she would have had to do, not something she was ever asked to do.

**A build or an edit must not turn "proposes a placement" into "files it", and must not drop the sentence about the load moving to whoever maintains the tree.** Series A teaches that a constraint moves when you exploit it; a chapter of this series declaring one eliminated would contradict its own companion.
