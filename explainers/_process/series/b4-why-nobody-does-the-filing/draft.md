# Draft — B4, *Why nobody does the filing*

Series B, chapter 4 of 5. Bead `reasoncommons-bnl.20`. Prose only, no visual direction. Follows [B3](../b3-give-every-claim-an-address/script.md), which ends on the placement being our reading of Maren's sentence, and an argument the tree can now hold.

---

**Key claim:** Structure takes effort, so it looks like a choice between easy and cumulative — but that rests on one assumption: that the person contributing has to do the filing.

---

If it is this useful, why is almost nobody working this way?

Because of what the last chapter quietly asked of Maren. To put her sentence where it belongs she would have had to find the tree, learn what a box and an arrow mean there, read enough of it to know which of the eight conditions her point bears on, phrase her objection in the grammar of necessity, and then defend that placement to the people who drew the thing.

Every one of those is reasonable. Together they are a morning's work before she has said anything. She is twenty-three, she has a dissertation, and she came to the forum because there was a conversation happening. She is not going to do the filing, and it would be strange of us to expect it.

This is not a problem with Maren, or with newcomers. Nobody joined a movement for civilizational renewal in order to fill in forms. The people already inside don't do it either: the research circle in chapter one takes minutes, which is the cheap version, and even that produced nothing the movement holds.

So it looks like a straight choice. Keep contributing easy — post what you think, in your own words, when you think it — and accept that the good parts dissolve into the scroll. Or make contribution structured, so it cumulates, and accept that most people will not pay the entry price, especially the newcomers you most want.

Both sides of that want the same thing: a group whose thinking actually compounds. And when two things you want look incompatible, it is usually worth hunting for the assumption that makes them look that way, rather than picking a side.

Here it is: **that the structure has to be put there by a person, and specifically by the person contributing, at the moment they contribute.**

Say it out loud and you can see it is not a law of nature. It is a description of what used to be affordable. Filing a claim means reading a sentence, working out what it asserts, and finding the place in an existing structure where that assertion belongs. Until very recently the only thing that could do that was a person who knew both the sentence and the structure — which meant the contributor, or a maintainer with time nobody has.

That assumption is no longer true in the age of AI. Reading a piece of prose against a structure and proposing where it fits is exactly the shape of thing current models are good at. Not perfectly, not unsupervised, and not yet well enough to trust with what the group holds — but well enough to do the reading, which is the part that was never going to scale.

The dilemma dissolves rather than being settled. Contribution stays as easy as posting on a forum, and the filing still gets done, by something that does not mind doing it.

This move — find the shared objective, find the assumption underneath the conflict, break the assumption — is itself a tool from the method, and there is a chapter about it in the companion series.
