# Draft — B2, *Nowhere to put it*

Series B, chapter 2 of 5. Bead `reasoncommons-bnl.16`. Prose only, no visual direction. Follows [B1](../b1-same-conversation/script.md), which ends on the paradox: the record is complete and the movement still cannot say what it concluded.

---

**Key claim:** Forums file by date and papers by paragraph, so a single claim has no address, and what you can't point at you can't improve.

---

So the record is not the problem. Everything Maren wrote is still on the forum. The circle's minutes are in a folder. The papers are published. Nothing is lost in the sense of being gone.

Ask a simpler question instead: where should her sentence have gone?

Not "where is it stored" — it is stored. Where does it *belong*? What is the thing in the movement that her sentence was about, that ought to look different now that she has said it?

There isn't one. That is the whole of the problem, and it is easy to miss because it looks like an absence rather than a fault.

The forum files by date. Date is the only question it can answer: what was said, and when. It cannot tell you what the movement currently holds about place, or which of its commitments Maren's sentence bears on, because it has no representation of what the movement holds. It is a log, and a log has no opinions.

Writing things up properly does not fix it. The movement has six or seven white papers, close to a hundred thousand words, and they are careful. But a claim buried in a paragraph on page forty has no address. You cannot point at it. And what you cannot point at, you cannot disagree with precisely, or improve, or version, or notice has drifted out of agreement with the same claim on page eleven of a different paper.

Watch what this does to the contribution. Maren had a specific objection to a specific commitment. To act on it she would have had to: find the paper, find the page, work out whether the sentence she was arguing with was load-bearing or throat-clearing, then write a post that reconstructed all of that for everybody else before she could get to her point. Nobody does this. She wrote one clear sentence instead, and one clear sentence has nowhere to attach, so it stayed a good comment in a thread.

**The most valuable thing a newcomer brings — a specific disagreement with a specific claim — is exactly the thing we are least able to receive.**

The word for what is missing is an address: a place a claim lives, that other claims can refer to, that you can watch change over time. Not a folder, not a tag. Something a sentence can be *attached to*, so that attaching it changes what the group holds.

We have addresses for code, and for money, and for law. We don't have them for reasoning, and nothing in the last decade of tooling has made us want them less.
