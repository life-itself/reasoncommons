# Whose Map Is It — visual script

**Stage 2 of 3.** The approved script from `03-script-v2.md`, unchanged, with the visuals
annotated against it. Where a line below differs from the script, the script wins and this
file is wrong.

## The world

One continuous surface: **a sheet of working paper with a map on it.** Every stage is
either that map, a part of it enlarged, or something happening to it. Nothing is a fresh
unrelated figure.

Register is the series register, settled in `02aef9c`: hand-drawn on paper. Same `#rough`
displacement filters, same Organic tokens via `scroller.css`, `--mark` reserved for *the
thing at issue at this moment* — which in this piece moves, deliberately, from the
throughput node, to the missing decision procedure, to the assumption, to the contributor.

Eleven pinned stages. Each needs a portrait arrangement; the default move, as in the
trilogy, is to stack and carry sideways relationships on a spine.

## What the drawings must carry that the prose does not

Three loads, and they are the reason this is an explainer and not an article:

1. **Nothing sits above the two definitions.** The prose says the group had no way to
   choose. The drawing shows two cards of equal weight with an empty dashed box above them.
   The absence is the point, and it is only visible as an absence in a picture.
2. **The sawtooth.** The prose says individual learning compounds and group knowing does
   not. The drawing shows one line climbing and another returning to the same baseline three
   times. The reader gets the shape before the sentence lands.
3. **The stack is fatal.** The prose lists four reasonable steps. The drawing makes them a
   staircase between a person and the map, and then shows the person not climbing it.

Everything else is support.

> In June, a group working on the Second Renaissance sat down to answer one question: how

>
> They had plenty to answer it with. Years of white papers, a research circle that meets

>

>

>

### S6 · The sawtooth — pinned, 4 steps

The second load-bearing panel. A time axis runs left to right across the full frame. Step 1
draws the individual line: a staircase climbing steadily, labelled *what a person takes
away*. Step 2 draws the group line: it rises to a peak, then falls back to the baseline.
Step 3 repeats that peak-and-fall twice more, at even spacing, each peak the same height as
the first — the sawtooth. Step 4 marks the three peaks with one label.

> A group has a passionate discussion and minds change — we need to be politically active,
> it is agreed. Two weeks later nothing has changed.
>
> Months later the same discussion happens again, from the beginning, because the conditions
> that produced it haven't changed either.
>
> Robert is right about people. Twenty years of his students prove it. Individual learning
> compounds.
>
> Group knowing, without something to hold it in, does not.

Then, on its own statement screen:

> Anyone who has been in this initiative more than a year has had the same conversation
> three times.

**What the drawing adds:** the reader sees the second line fail to hold height before the
sentence explains it — and the three identical peaks are the recognition the whole piece is
aiming at.

*Portrait:* same drawing, narrower; the axis keeps its full width because the repetition is
the point. This is the one stage that stays landscape-shaped, scaled down.

### S8 · The stack, and the person at the bottom — pinned, 5 steps

The third load-bearing panel. Drawing: the map sits top right. A figure stands bottom left.
Between them a staircase of four risers is drawn one per step, each labelled.

> What did touching the trees actually require that week?
>
> Read five diagrams. Learn a grammar — sufficiency, necessity, the etiquette of a
> legitimate reservation. Locate your point among a hundred nodes. Then defend it to the
> people who wrote them. *(risers appear across two steps)*
>
> Every one of those steps is reasonable. The stack is fatal.

Then the figure. Their sentence appears as a small card beside them, drifts upward, does not
attach to anything, and fades:

> Somewhere out there is a person who posts, in public, that all this metacrisis talk floats
> free of any actual place — that the work should be rooted in a watershed. It is a real
> objection to a specific claim in the current reality tree.
>
> Nobody rebuffs them. They are simply not heard, because being heard required reading five
> diagrams first.

Final step: the single figure multiplies into a faint crowd filling the lower band.

> The movement's own list of symptoms has a line for this. *Many people resonate with the
> ideas; few enter sustained practice.* That is the same person, several thousand times.

**The crowd does the arithmetic the prose refuses to.** Do not label it with a number.

### S9 · The cloud — pinned, 5 steps

SR's real conflict, redrawn from the working diagram. Objective box at left: *a group whose
thinking compounds toward its goal*. Two need boxes centre: *open, spontaneous, relational
conversation* and *focus, clarity, efforts that add up*. Two want boxes right: *keep
contribution unstructured* and *structure every contribution into the trees*. Arrows from
wants to needs to objective. Then the lightning between the two wants. Then the dashed
assumption box. Then the crack through it.

> Some people love the trees — the focus, the clarity, the feeling of effort finally adding
> up.
>
> Others love the conversation exactly as it is, spontaneous and alive, and are rightly
> suspicious of anything that interrupts it in order to file it.
>
> Both wants serve real needs. Both needs serve the same goal.
>
> The move here is not to pick a side. It is to ask what would have to be true for these two
> to be genuinely opposed, and then to write that assumption down and look at it.
>
> Here it is. *Structure has to be added by the contributor, at the moment of contributing.*

Then, as a statement screen:

> That is the only thing holding the conflict together, and it is not true.

Both sides of this cloud must be drawn with equal care, for the same reason Robert's card
is never faded.

### S10 · Separate the acts — pinned, 4 steps

Drawing: three horizontal bands, drawn top to bottom as the steps land. Band one, the
conversation: a loose continuous scribble that runs off both edges — it does not start or
stop. Band two, the bridge: a small listening mark beneath it, with a dotted line lifting
one fragment of the scribble out. Band three, the map: the lattice, with a rotating circle
of three small marks beside one node. The fragment travels down the dotted line and becomes
a node. A thin line then runs back up to the contributor.

> Let the conversation be a conversation. Nothing interrupts it.
>
> Something else listens afterwards and proposes a place — this sentence supports that node,
> this one challenges another.
>
> A small rotating circle decides what merges, because one person quietly deciding what the
> map says is the thing that started all of this.
>
> The cost of contributing falls to one sentence, said where you were already saying it.

The return line is the last thing drawn in the piece. It is what the person in S8 never got.

### CLOSE — flow, then statements

> A month ago the question was: what is our throughput?
>
> It is now: by what process does a group of people keep a shared map of its thinking true?
>
> That is a smaller question and a harder one, and everything else in the initiative is
> waiting behind it.
>
> What comes next is not a strategy. It is a protocol. How a proposal enters the map, who
> hears the objections, who merges, and how the person who offered the sentence gets to
> watch it land.
>
> It is dull work. *(statement)*
>
> Nobody here was ever short of ideas. *(statement, full size)*

No final drawing. The last image the reader keeps is the return line.

## Build notes for stage 3

- Reuse `scroller.css` and `scroller.js` unchanged. No new engine, no interactions.
- Two `feDisplacementMap` filters as per the trilogy; new seeds so the paper doesn't look
  photocopied from piece 3.
- Recurring geometry — the chain in S1/S4, the lattice in S5/S7/S10, the cards in S2/S3/S7 —
  should be drawn by shared helpers at the same coordinates each time. The recognition is
  the reason it is one world.
- Progressive reveals inside a stage use `window.onStep`, as in piece 3; whole-layer
  reveals use `data-layer`.
- Eleven stages is more than any trilogy piece. If the build runs long, the two that can be
  cut to flow prose without losing an argument are S2 (the first definition) and S7
  (throughput, second attempt). S3, S6 and S8 cannot be cut.

## What the build changed (recorded after stage 3)

Ten pinned stages shipped, not eleven — S1's cold open and the title card merged as
designed, and nothing was cut. Prose is unchanged from `03-script-v2.md`. Three
departures from this file, all made at the screen:

- **The crack in the cloud is a step, not a statement screen.** S9's closing line
  ("the only thing holding the conflict together, and it is not true") lands as step
  `k6` inside the pinned stage, so the reader sees the assumption break rather than
  reading about it on a separate screen.
- **The first definition lives in the drawing, not in a text step.** S2 has two steps;
  the definition itself is written into the card. The form asks for the drawing to
  carry what it can, and a definition on a card is a drawing.
- **Every stage was re-laid-out once against a browser.** The first pass letterboxed:
  a viewBox 880×400 in a 1100×800 frame renders at 1100×500 and parks the drawing in a
  middle band, so half the height went unused and captions sat on the diagrams. All
  stages now use a viewBox near the frame's own 1.375 aspect. The sawtooth (S6) and the
  staircase (S8) needed genuinely different portrait compositions rather than a scale-down.
