# Somewhere to Put It

*The Second Renaissance produces more good thinking than it can hold on to.*

---

The Second Renaissance is a project for civilizational renewal, gathered around the
Life Itself collective. Its claim is that our crises are not separate cracks but
symptoms of a paradigm in decline, and that a wiser one has to be consciously built
rather than waited for.

It is largely volunteer, and its thesis is not finished. It is not supposed to be —
the whole premise is that it gets better as more people think about it. But
everything the project holds in common is either a document somebody wrote or a
conversation somebody remembers.

That is a problem of a particular kind. Not a shortage of intelligence, or of
goodwill, or of ideas.

A shortage of somewhere to put them.

---

Start with the conversation, because that is where most of the thinking happens.

A research circle meets every week. By any honest measure the conversations are
good — better than most of what gets published on the subject.

Something gets decided in the room. The work has been too abstract; it needs to be
rooted in actual places, with actual watersheds and neighbours.

Everybody leaves agreeing.

Two weeks later the agreement has nobody's name on it and nothing has changed.

Months later the same conversation happens again, from the beginning, because the
conditions that produced it are exactly where they were.

Anyone who has been around this project for more than a year has had that
conversation three times.

---

It would be easy to read that as a failure of follow-through, and it isn't.

The people in the room learned something and kept it. They are better at this than
they were. Individual understanding compounds; twenty years of anyone's students
will show you that.

What doesn't compound is the group's version. That has nowhere to live except in the
people who were in the room, and people leave rooms.

Agreement that survives only in the people who were present is a rumour.

---

So ask the plainer question: when somebody has a good thought, where is it supposed
to go?

The forum thread is filed by the day it was posted. Nothing in it is filed by what
it is about.

The papers are prose, and a claim buried in the middle of a paragraph has no
address. You cannot point at it. If you cannot point at it, you cannot object to it
precisely — and a vague objection to a long document is one nobody can act on, so
nobody makes it.

Which means the most valuable thing a newcomer brings — a specific, load-bearing
disagreement — is the thing the project is least equipped to receive.

---

Earlier this year the project tried something about that.

It took its own written corpus and rewrote the strategy as five linked diagrams: the
goal and what it depends on, what is currently blocking it, the tensions underneath
that, the future being aimed at, and the order things have to happen in.

The point is not that they are diagrams. It is that a claim in a diagram has an
address, and the lines between claims are claims too — *this one is necessary for
that one* — so the reasoning is exposed and not just the conclusions.

You can see the whole thesis at once. Roughly eight things have to be true at the
same time for a Second Renaissance to be more than an idea: people have to understand
the crisis as one crisis; there has to be a vision worth wanting; it has to become
practice and not just agreement; small dense groups have to make that practice
livable; and so on, up to institutions and the wise navigation of collapse.

*The method behind the five shapes is forty years old and was invented for factories.
A companion piece covers it properly. Nothing here depends on it.*

---

Two things change when the thesis is laid out like that, and they are worth naming
separately.

The first is that *where do I fit* stops being a feeling and becomes a place you can
point at. Bioregional practice isn't a mood you share with the project; it is a
condition sitting under one of those eight, and if it is missing, it is missing in an
identifiable spot.

The second is that disagreement gets small. You do not have to reject the Second
Renaissance in order to think it is wrong about how pockets form. You can attack one
arrow and leave the rest standing — and if the arrow gives way, everyone can see
precisely what else moves.

That second one matters more than it sounds. A group that can only agree or defect
has one setting. A group that can revise a specific claim can be wrong in public and
keep going.

---

Now the honest objection, which is not a small one.

Ask what it actually takes, today, to change a single claim on those maps. Read five
diagrams. Learn a grammar — what makes a cause sufficient, what makes a condition
necessary, how to raise a reservation properly. Locate your point among a hundred
boxes. Then defend it to the people who drew them.

Every one of those steps is reasonable. The stack is fatal.

The maps fix the forum's problem by importing a worse one. They ask the person
contributing to do the filing, and almost nobody will. The project's own list of
symptoms already has the line for it: *many people resonate with the ideas; few enter
sustained practice.*

---

Underneath sits a conflict every community that gives itself structure runs into
eventually.

Some people want the focus — the clarity, the sense that effort is finally adding up.
Others want the conversation exactly as it is, spontaneous and relational, and are
rightly suspicious of anything that interrupts a good exchange in order to file it.

Both wants serve real needs, and both needs serve the same goal. So the move is not
to pick a side. It is to find the assumption that makes them look opposed, write it
down, and check whether it is true.

Here it is. *Structure has to be added by the contributor, at the moment of
contributing.*

That is the only thing holding the conflict together, and it is not true.

---

Let the conversation be a conversation. Nothing interrupts it. You post the
paragraph where you were going to post it anyway, in the register you were going to
use.

Afterwards, something reads the thread against the maps and proposes places: this
sentence supports that condition, this one challenges that arrow, this one is a
claim the maps do not have anywhere to put yet.

A rotating handful of people decide what actually merges, because one person quietly
deciding what the map says is how you end up with a map nobody trusts.

The cost of contributing falls back to one sentence, said where you were already
saying it. The filing is somebody else's job, and it is not a person's job.

---

Then the forum thread is doing two things at once. It is still a conversation. It is
also the mechanism by which the thesis changes.

Go back to the person who posted on Tuesday. Nine replies, a good exchange, and one
of their sentences ends up hanging off a claim in the current reality tree as a
standing objection with their name on it.

They have not joined a mailing list. They have altered what the project believes, in
a spot anyone can find.

---

None of this is finished. The maps are provisional and wrong in several places; the
thing that reads the threads is a prototype; the rules for merging are three
sentences long and have never been tested by anyone annoyed.

But the maps are public, and being wrong in public is the point.

Find the claim you disagree with.

The Second Renaissance has never been short of thinking.

It has been short of somewhere to put it.

## Sources and method

The five trees — goal, current reality, conflict resolution, future reality,
prerequisite — exist as written documents and are the source of every diagram in this
piece; nothing here is an invented illustration. The eight conditions are the
critical success factors of the goal tree. The symptom quoted is the first
undesirable effect of the current reality tree. The conflict between spontaneous
conversation and structure, and the assumption holding it together, are redrawn from
the working conflict-resolution diagram.

The research-circle conversation and the person who posts on a Tuesday are
composites, drawn from the pattern rather than from one occasion. Nobody is quoted
here.

The onramp described near the end is a working prototype, not a finished system: at
the time of writing the matching is not good enough to run unattended, and the merge
rules exist only as a draft. It is described as what the structure makes possible,
which is the honest tense for it.

This page is itself a first draft of a node. Object to it, and watch what happens.
