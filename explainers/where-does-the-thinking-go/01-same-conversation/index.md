<p class="ch-lede">Groups keep having the same good conversation, because what one person learns compounds but what the group agrees keeps resetting to zero.</p>

A week after joining the Second Renaissance forum, Maren posts this:

> The theory of change seems to float free of any actual place. You should start in a neighbourhood somebody actually lives in.

Maren is invented — a composite of the newcomers we keep meeting. Everything that happens to her post is not.

Nine people reply. Two disagree, and disagree well. One links a paper. One says he has been making this argument for a year and nobody picks it up. Then the thread is forty posts from the top, and Maren, who had been reading the forum daily, stops opening it.

The Second Renaissance is a movement thinking in public about how a civilization in trouble might renew itself. Like any such movement it is meant to get sharper as more people join. A year after Maren's post, none of its white papers say anything different, and the strategy documents are the documents they were.

Her point was not new to the movement, either.

A research circle meets every week, and three times in the last two years it has worked its way to the same conclusion: that the strategy has to be grounded somewhere specific. Once it got there by arguing about the theory head-on. Once it came at it sideways, through why a local chapter had folded. The third time, nobody in the room knew the question had been worked through twice already, so it was worked through again from the beginning. Three good meetings, three agreements, and the third one started from nothing.

What the man who has been arguing this for a year has, the circle does not. He carried the point for twelve months: he can state it faster now, he knows the two objections, he has a case ready. That is what it means for understanding to compound — you keep what you worked out and start from there. The circle keeps nothing. It has no year; it has a series of first attempts, because what it agreed lives only in whoever was in the room.

**What one person learns, they keep. What a group agrees, it loses — so every few months the same conversation starts again from nothing.**

This is not a failure of this movement in particular. It is what usually happens to a group that thinks together and then has to remember.

And it is strange, because nobody involved was careless. The circle takes minutes. The forum keeps every post. Maren's sentence is still there, exactly where she left it, and anyone can read it today. The record is complete, and the movement still cannot tell you what it concluded the first two times.
