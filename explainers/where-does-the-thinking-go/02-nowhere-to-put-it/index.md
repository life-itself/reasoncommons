<p class="ch-eyebrow"><a href="../">Where does the thinking go?</a> · Chapter 2 of 5</p>

<p class="ch-lede">Forums file by date and papers by paragraph, so a single claim has no address — and what you can't point at, you can't improve.</p>

So the record is not the problem. The question is where Maren's sentence should have *gone* — not where it is stored, but what it was about, and what ought to look different now that she has said it.

What is missing is an address: a place a claim lives, that other claims can point at, and that changes when somebody changes it. The movement has none, for two ordinary reasons.

The first is the forum. It can tell you where a sentence was said. It cannot tell you what the movement now holds about place, because it has no representation of what the movement holds — searching it returns occurrences, not a position. The circle's minutes are the same case, and the cleaner one: the third time it reached that conclusion, the conclusion became another dated document. Nothing the movement holds was any different on the Friday than it had been on the Monday.

The second is the papers. There are six or seven of them, close to a hundred thousand words, and they are careful. But a claim buried in a paragraph on page forty has no address either. You cannot point at it. People have of course built things for this — wikis, tags, knowledge bases — and what took hold still files by document or by tag, which is to say by where a claim was written down rather than by what it asserts.

**What you cannot point at, you cannot disagree with precisely, improve, or notice has drifted.**

That last one has a cost worth spelling out. Somewhere in those hundred thousand words, the same commitment is stated more than once, by different people, at different times. Do the statements still agree? Nobody can tell you. That is not an admission of sloppiness; it is that there is no place where such a disagreement would show up. The claim on page forty and the claim on page eleven of another paper are not linked to each other, because neither is a thing — each is a passage.

It costs Maren more directly. To act on her objection she would have had to find the paper, find the page, work out whether the sentence she was arguing with was load-bearing or throat-clearing, then reconstruct all of that for everyone else before reaching her point. Nobody does this. She wrote one clear sentence instead. One clear sentence has nowhere to attach, so it stayed a good comment in a thread. What a newcomer is best placed to give is the thing we are worst at receiving.

We do know how to do this elsewhere. A section of a statute has a citation. Everything that relies on it cites that citation, and when the section is amended, everything pointing at it now points at the new text. That is exactly what an address gives you. Reasoning does not have one.
