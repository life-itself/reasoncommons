# Thinking in Systems

*A series in three parts*

Almost every organisation is busy. Rather fewer are getting anywhere. This is
about the difference — and about a way of thinking, invented for factories, that
turns out to apply to almost anything you are responsible for.

Each part is a scrolling piece: the drawings build as you read. Best on a
reasonably wide screen.

---

### Part one · [The Wrong Queue](01-bottleneck/index.html)

A clinic where sixty people are waiting and everybody knows where the problem
is. They are all wrong — and what they get wrong is the most reliably
counterintuitive thing about how systems behave.

---

### Part two · [The Arrows Nobody Checks](02-thinking-made-visible/index.html)

Five intelligent people, five confident and incompatible answers. Why arguing
doesn't settle it, why most diagrams are decoration, and the eight questions
that turn a picture into an argument.

---

### Part three · [Five Shapes](03-five-shapes/index.html)

The method itself: five questions any real change has to answer, the
differently shaped answers they need, and why its author quietly retired one of
his own tools.
