# Editorial critique — *Everybody's Busy*, script v1

Read as a commissioning editor at a serious non-fiction outlet would read it: does it earn
its length, does it move, would a stranger finish it, and is it *true* in the way good
non-fiction has to be true — not just accurate, but not overselling.

**Verdict:** the argument is sound and the spine is strong. The prose is competent and
occasionally very good. But it is **an explanation, not a story**, and it has three defects
that would stop me running it as-is. One of them is serious.

---

## The serious one

### 1 · Nobody is in it. Nobody suffers. There are no stakes.

Sixty people are waiting. Forty of them will not be seen today. The piece never says so.

It never says what happens to them, never follows one of them home, never converts twenty
patients a day into the thing it actually is: **roughly ten thousand people a year who came
to a clinic and did not get help — while the building was full of people working flat out.**

That is the emotional and moral centre of this piece and it is entirely absent. Without it,
the clinic is a worked example — a maths problem with a roof on it — and the reader has no
reason to feel anything. ProPublica can be abstract about carbon because the stakes are
pre-loaded in the reader's head. Nobody arrives at our piece already caring about clinic
throughput. **We have to give them a reason, and we have one sitting unused.**

There is a second prize here. The craft skill flagged that we had no Comparison Ladder
because our subject has no large quantities. **That was wrong.** We do:

> 40 people turned away today → 200 this week → **about 10,000 a year** → and the first
> 8 of them a day could have been seen for **nothing**, by moving two hours of paperwork
> off one desk.

That is the strongest thing in the material and v1 doesn't use it.

**Fix:** name the receptionist. Give one waiting patient one sentence of specificity —
enough to be a person, not enough to be a case study. Land the annual number. Do all of it
without sentiment; the arithmetic is doing the work.

---

## The two structural ones

### 2 · The concept is never named, so the reader leaves without a word for it

v1 calls it "the limit", "the limiting part", "the thing that's stuck" — and then, in the
final section, suddenly says **"find the constraint"**, a term never introduced.

Either commit to the word early, once the reader has *felt* it (right after the "still
twenty" sequence is the natural place), or never use it. Right now it does the worst of
both: withholds the vocabulary the reader needs to think with, then deploys it as though
it were established.

I'd name it. A reader who finishes with a word can use the idea on Monday. A reader who
finishes with a feeling cannot.

### 3 · The title promises a different piece

"Everybody's Busy" promises the busy-trap argument. That argument is real and it's good —
but it is one section in the middle. The spine of the piece is *the queue is not where the
problem is*.

Retitle to match the spine, or restructure so the busy trap is the spine. Don't ship the
mismatch.

---

## Line and paragraph notes

**Cut, and this is not close:**

> This is not really a story about a clinic.

Telling the reader what to conclude before they've concluded it. The whole §6 sequence
(funnel, team, parent) exists to make exactly this point *by demonstration*. Trust it.

**The three lateral examples are a list, and lists kill momentum.**

They arrive immediately after the most actionable passage in the piece (exploit →
subordinate → elevate) and flatten it into enumeration. The parent is by far the best of
the three and it's buried third.

Cut the funnel — it's the most jargon-adjacent and least universal. Keep the team (one
sentence) and the parent (give it room). Two examples read as a pattern; three read as a
list.

**"Almost everyone says yes. It feels less like a claim than like arithmetic."**

The second sentence is doing a clever thing clumsily. *"Almost everyone says yes. It
doesn't even feel like an opinion."*

**The four example rules break frame at the last moment.**

> *Orders go in monthly, to get the discount.*

Procurement, not clinical — it drops us out of the clinic exactly when §8 needs us inside
it. Replace with a rule from the same world. *Referrals are reviewed on Thursdays.*

**Signposting is doing work the structure should do.**

"Now the question that decides whether…", "Back to the clinic.", "You may have noticed
that…", "Which produces a fact that is…". Four throat-clears. In a scroll piece each of
these costs a screen. Cut at least three; let the hard cut do the work.

**"Busy is not the same as stuck."**

Best line in the script, and it's buried at the end of a paragraph. Isolate it.

**The ending is a trailer, not an ending.**

> So people invented one.

That's a promo for part two. The reference piece ends on a flat verdict — *"Carbon capture
and storage remains elusive."* — and then hands off to sources. Ours should land the
argument, and let the link to part two be a link, not the last line.

Candidate: end on the guessing. *They guess. Then they spend a year finding out.* That's
the actual indictment, and it's ours.

---

## What's working — don't lose it in revision

- **The cold open.** Three sentences, then *"They are all wrong."* Sympathetic setup, flat demolition. Don't touch it.
- **The "still twenty" sequence.** Four repetitions, each landing the same number. It is the piece's best argument and it makes it structurally, not rhetorically.
- **"Frustrated sitting down instead of standing up."** Concrete, mildly funny, earns its place.
- **"She was busy absorbing the anger that something else was generating."** The whole thesis in one sentence.
- **Cutting the chain metaphor was right.** v1 dropped Goldratt's chain, and the piece is better for it — the clinic already demonstrates single-limit behaviour, and a metaphor that restates a demonstration is padding. Resist the urge to put it back for canonical reasons.
- **Deriving the busy-trap instead of quoting Dettmer.** v1 works it out from the clinic's own numbers rather than appealing to authority. Much stronger. Keep the quote out.
- **"If you cannot tolerate that on a dashboard, none of this is available to you."** Unusually honest. Most business writing wouldn't concede the cost.

---

## Priority order for v2

1. **Add the stakes.** Name the receptionist; one patient made real; land ~10,000 a year; pair it with the free fix. *(the serious one)*
2. **Name the concept** once, early, after it's been felt.
3. **Retitle** to match the spine.
4. **Cut the funnel example**; give the parent room.
5. **Cut "this is not really a story about a clinic"** and three of the four signposts.
6. **Re-end** on the guess, not the trailer.
7. Line fixes: the arithmetic sentence; the procurement rule; isolate *busy is not the same as stuck*.

Length after: roughly neutral. Cuts 5 and 4 pay for addition 1.
