# Voice guide — explainers v2

Derived from Rufus's hand rewrite of the 2R talk script on 2026-09-04 (commits `6df4a36`, `80e3858`, `d4a1c37` on `talk/2r-research-group/script.md`). Run `git show --word-diff=plain <sha> -- talk/2r-research-group/script.md` to see every edit. The earlier AI drafts were in the register the current explainers are written in; the rewrite is the target.

## The direction of every edit

1. **Plain over literary.** Cut the aphorism, keep the claim. Endings that land on a turn of phrase get replaced by what actually happens.
   - ~~The thinking stayed in the room, and people leave rooms.~~ → The thinking stayed in the room.
   - ~~A week later it was nowhere. No note that changed what we do. No thread that carried it forward.~~ → A week later it was largely forgotten. Nothing changed in any of the white papers.
2. **"We", not "I" or the narrator.** Speak as the people doing the work, inviting the reader in.
   - ~~I have been trying to answer that~~ → We have been trying to answer that.
3. **Say the thing explicitly.** Don't make the reader infer the concept from a scene; tell them what it is, then show it.
   - ~~You take the top claim. You break it into the sub-claims it depends on…~~ → A tree helps you structure a thesis. Its root, its foundational node, is the top claim. Then you break that down into the sub-claims it depends on…
   - ~~So that is what this talk is about.~~ → Could we do better? And how? … How can we provide structure and process to our discourse so that it **accumulates**?
4. **Concrete and specific over dramatic.** Real places the thinking gets lost, not stylised ones.
   - ~~because everything that produced it was still sitting where it had been~~ → because everything that produced it was stuck in a zoom call or an old thread on the forum.
5. **Name the lineage.** Credit the ideas: Goldratt's Theory of Constraints, Minto's Pyramid Principle, the Logical Thinking Process. Don't hide the method behind "a forty-year-old method".
6. **Name AI as the enabler.** The turn in the argument is that structure no longer has to be added by a person at the moment of contributing. Say so directly.
   - ~~That assumption is not true. That is more or less the whole talk.~~ → **That assumption is no longer true in the age of AI.** That is what makes a reason commons possible.
7. **Bold the one key claim** of a section. One per section, not decoration.
8. **Honest, calibrated hedges.** Don't overclaim, and acknowledge the other side generously.
   - ~~I know "goal" and "progress" are loaded words in this room~~ → "Goal" and "progress" … may be loaded words. … We would agree.
   - Cut credibility-signalling asides (~~I did not build it for the talk.~~).
9. **Frame tensions as dilemmas that dissolve.** "It looks like you have a dilemma: keep it accessible or make it structured so it cumulates. But fortunately there is one dissolvable assumption underneath…" This is the evaporating cloud, told in plain words.
10. **Warmth and a little lightness.** "It's pull requests for discourse 🙂". Close on an image that invites (gardeners and permaculturists who plan and use reason) rather than a punchline or a command.
11. **Friendly to newcomers.** Name the cost of structure for new contributors — the people you most want to join — and keep accessibility, ease and joy as goals, not obstacles.

## Checklist for a script

- [ ] Could a reader who has never heard of LTP say, after this chapter, the one or two things it set out to say?
- [ ] Is each concept stated plainly before (or as) it is illustrated?
- [ ] Any sentence whose job is to sound good rather than to say something? Cut it.
- [ ] "We" voice; lineage credited where a method is used; AI named where it is the enabler.
- [ ] One bolded key claim at most per section.
- [ ] Every hedge honest, no overclaim, the opposing view stated fairly.
- [ ] 300–600 words.
