## The reader

Someone who runs or cares about something complex — a team, an organization, a movement — and has felt that effort isn't adding up. Smart, busy, not a management-science reader, often on a phone. Arrives from the Second Renaissance forum, a talk, or a link. Has never heard of the Theory of Constraints or the Logical Thinking Process.

## What they should be able to say afterwards

1. **Most effort in a system doesn't move the goal; one constraint does, and it is rarely where people are busiest, and often a rule.**
2. **Arguing rarely settles what the constraint is. Drawing the cause and effect, and checking each arrow with a few fair questions, gives you a much better chance.**
3. **Five questions any real change has to answer — what should be happening, why isn't it, why hasn't it been fixed, will the fix work, what do we do first — each answered by a differently shaped tree.**
4. **A group's thinking only accumulates if each claim has an address, and AI now makes it affordable to give it one without making contributors do the filing.**

B carries 4; A carries 1–3.

## Shape

Two short series, each readable in one sitting (~15 minutes), cross-linked. **Series B first on the index**: the people arriving now come from the Second Renaissance, B is shorter, has a person in it and ends on an ask. A is the go-deeper path. B teaches the little method it needs (necessity, in two sentences) rather than depending on A.

## Series B — Where does the thinking go? (5 chapters)

Running character: Maren (composite, said so). Every tree shown is the Second Renaissance's real one.

1. **The same conversation, again and again** — **Groups keep having the same good conversation, because what one person learns compounds but what the group agrees keeps resetting to zero.** · Maren's post arrives, gathers nine replies, greys into the thread (one looping animation); the research circle reaching the same point three times; the person's staircase vs the group's peaks. The movement is introduced *after* Maren, in two plain sentences.
2. **Nowhere to put it** — **Forums file by date and papers by paragraph, so a single claim has no address, and what you can't point at you can't improve.** · "A claim on page forty has no address." The newcomer's specific disagreement is the thing we're least able to receive.
3. **Give every claim an address** — **A tree of claims gives every claim, and every link between them, a place you can point to — so Maren's suggestion has somewhere to land.** · What a tree is, said plainly; necessity in two sentences ("to have this, we need that"); the real 2R goal tree growing from its root (animation); Maren's card landing as a missing condition under "small dense groups". Link to A5 for the method.
4. **Why nobody does the filing** — **Structure takes effort, so it looks like a choice between easy and cumulative — but that rests on one assumption: that the person contributing has to do the filing.** · The stairs; the dilemma in words, no cloud diagram; the assumption; **"no longer true in the age of AI."** Link to A7.
5. **Talk first, file later** — **AI reads the conversation afterwards and proposes where each point belongs; people decide what goes in.** · The worked example from Demo C: a real AI reading of rehearsal contributions against the real tree — e.g. Maya's "the goal is too broad to choose by" proposed as a challenge to the top goal, with its confidence and an alternative reading; one contribution the tree can't place yet. Stated plainly as rehearsal material and a prototype. One sentence on the tree pointing the forum back at earlier threads. Close: the status, the invitation ("find the claim you disagree with, or the one nobody is working on").

## Series A — Why fixes don't stick (8 chapters)

Running example: Ashfield clinic (composite, arithmetic checkable). **The open question that carries A:** Dr Adeyemi loses two hours a day to admin — *why?* Asked in A2, answered by the current reality tree in A6, resolved with Maria in A8.

### Part one · Find the constraint

1. **Sixty people waiting** — **How much any system achieves is set by one constraint, and it is almost never where people are busiest.** · Maria's desk (busiest, irrelevant) vs the doctor (twenty a day for four years). The clinic's goal is patients seen, well — throughput said plainly. Figure: five stations, patients flowing and piling before the doctor (animation). One line of lineage: Goldratt, the Theory of Constraints.
2. **Squeeze before you spend** — **Before paying to expand the constraint, get everything you can out of it — and expect it to move once you do.** · The one hire at the front desk makes a longer queue; the doctor's day has two hours of admin; give them back and the same day sees twenty-six, for nothing — *but why is the admin there?* (left open). Ends with the five focusing steps as one small picture: find it, squeeze it, make everything serve it, expand it, go again.
3. **The rule nobody can see** — **The stubborn constraint is usually a rule, and because everyone experiences it as normal, five sensible people will give you five different answers about what's wrong.** · Invisible rules (no pile of work in front of a rule); five staff, five confident causes. The hinge into Part two: you need a way to lay the reasoning out.

### Part two · Think in trees

4. **An arrow is a claim you can check** — **Every arrow in a diagram is a claim, read one of two ways — "this is needed for that" or "this causes that" — and anyone may fairly ask of it: do I understand it, is it true, is it the real cause, is it enough on its own?** · A tiny tree first (two boxes and an arrow from the clinic), then the two readings, then four questions; fire needs fuel, heat *and* oxygen. The full eight Categories of Legitimate Reservation in a footnote. Lineage: Dettmer's Logical Thinking Process named here.
5. **What should be happening? The goal tree** — **Start from the goal and ask what must be true for it, then what each of those needs: a tree read top-down with "in order to… we need…".** · The clinic's goal tree grows from its root (animation). This is the tree B3 links to.
6. **Why isn't it happening? The current reality tree** — **The many things going wrong trace back through cause and effect to a few root causes, and the deepest one is the constraint.** · Undesirable effects at the top, arrows converging — on the countersignature rule. The open question from A2 is answered: that's where the admin comes from. Say the bridge line: the root cause the tree finds is the constraint Part one was looking for.
7. **Why hasn't somebody fixed it? The cloud** — **A problem persists when fixing it seems to cost something else we need; name the assumption behind that conflict and it can often be dissolved.** · The countersignature exists for safety; dropping it seems to risk safety; the assumption (only the doctor can check a script) cracks. Figure: the five-box cloud, simplified.
8. **Walk it forward** — **Before acting, check that the fix produces what you want without new harm, then work out what's in the way and in what order.** · Future reality tree in one picture (ready notes need forty minutes of somebody's morning — Maria's); prerequisites in a sentence. Maria sees her referral backlog and the countersignature are the same jam from two ends of the corridor. Close: the five questions as one picture; "keeping trees like this alive across a whole group is the expensive part — that's the other series" → B.
