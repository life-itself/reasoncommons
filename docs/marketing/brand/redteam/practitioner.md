# Red team: the TOC / LTP practitioner

Point of view: I have facilitated CRTs, clouds and PRTs for clients for years. I have read Goldratt and Dettmer closely and I have watched people "simplify" the Thinking Processes into boxes-and-arrows that skip the logic. I want this to work, because I have always believed the TP belongs outside factories. But this draft would make me close the tab in the first paragraph and warn colleagues off it.

## 1. Accuracy

### "Forty-year-old method"

> "a free, open AI skill, and the forty-year-old method behind it"

> "The method has worked for forty years at roughly $2k a day."

Wrong unless you mean TOC in general. *The Goal* (1984) is about 42 years old, but it is a production-scheduling story, not the Thinking Processes. The TP as a set of logic trees dates from the early 1990s (the Jonah programme; *It's Not Luck*, 1994): about 32–35 years. "The Logical Thinking Process", by that name and with the Goal Tree, is Dettmer's 2007 book: under 20 years. The site names the method as LTP, so "forty-year-old" attaches to the wrong thing. A practitioner will notice in the first minute, and it costs you the credibility the line was meant to buy. Fix: "a method from the Theory of Constraints, refined over three decades", or "Goldratt's Thinking Processes, developed since the early 1990s".

### "Six trees"

> "a facilitator holding six trees in their head" (§3, practitioner row)

> "the method asks you to hold six linked trees and keep them all consistent" (narrative, idea 2)

Six is Dettmer's LTP count (Goal Tree, CRT, Evaporating Cloud, FRT, PRT, Transition Tree). Goldratt's original TP has five, and Goldratt later moved to Strategy & Tactics trees. The glossary gets this right ("began as ... *Thinking Processes* (five trees); Dettmer ... added the Goal Tree"), so the positioning is less careful than your own glossary. Two further problems. The Evaporating Cloud is not a tree, and practitioners will say so. More important, nobody does a full six-tree pass on most problems: the cloud alone is the workhorse, and many engagements are a CRT plus cloud. Your own skill says so ("there is no mandatory six-tree sequence"). Do not market the six-tree burden as the problem when the skill itself rejects the six-tree sequence.

### "Trees of change / trees of action"

> "A tree of change says what we are trying to achieve, what has to be true for it and what is in the way; a tree of action says what we do about it, in what order."

This is your vocabulary, not the method's, and it is fine as plain language, but do not present it as if it came from LTP. The canonical framing is Goldratt's three questions: *what to change? what to change to? how to cause the change?* Your two-way split loses the middle one. Where does the Future Reality Tree go: "change" or "action"? It is the step where an injection is tested for sufficiency and for negative branches, and that step is what separates the TP from a to-do list. `/ltp` says "the first trees are the tree of change ... the last are the tree of action" and leaves the FRT ambiguous. A practitioner will read this as the simplification I worry about: problem, then plan, with the logic test in the middle gone. Fix: either keep three ("what's wrong, what would fix it, how we get there") or say plainly that "change/action" is your shorthand and map each LTP tree to it in the guide, with the FRT and negative-branch reservation named.

### Cost claim (~$2k/day)

> "it took an expert at around $2k a day, so it was used where a $10M factory justified the bill"

> "hire an expert and only a factory can afford it"

The figure is unsourced, and your own §6 says so; it should not be in the positioning statement or the competitive frame until it is. It also misdescribes the market. TOC day rates vary widely. The real cost has never been the day rate; it is the training time (a Jonah course was two weeks) and the hours of facilitated sessions per tree. The factory line is factually wrong too. TP has been used in healthcare, government, the military, education and non-profits for decades. TOC for Education has taught clouds, branches and ambitious-target trees to schoolchildren in dozens of countries. "Only a factory can afford it" will read to anyone in the TOC world as not knowing the field.

### Cognitive-load claim

> "the method asks you to hold six linked trees and keep them all consistent as things change — a grasp very few people can sustain"

This diagnoses the wrong difficulty. Practitioners do not hold trees in their heads. We put them on walls and in Flying Logic, and keeping them on paper is easy. The hard parts are:

- **Logical discipline.** Keeping sufficiency logic (CRT, FRT, TrT) separate from necessity logic (Goal Tree, cloud, PRT), and scrutinising every arrow against the Categories of Legitimate Reservation: clarity, entity existence, causality existence, cause insufficiency, additional cause, cause–effect reversal, predicted effect existence, tautology.
- **Verbalisation.** Getting a group to state an entity as one clear, observable statement.
- **Buy-in.** The layers of resistance: agreement on the problem, on the direction of the solution, that it works, that its negative branches can be handled, and on overcoming the obstacles.

Kids in TOCfE manage the logic tools fine. Groups fail at scrutiny and buy-in, not at storage. If the pitch is "AI holds the trees for you", the practitioner hears "it solves the easy part". The stronger, true claim (see §3) is that AI can act as a patient scrutiniser who raises a reservation on every arrow without the facilitator becoming the group's antagonist.

## 2. Respect

> "unlike documents, threads, argument-mapping tools or consultants"

> "Consultants. The method has worked for forty years at roughly $2k a day. We make the same rigour cost a conversation, and leave the tree with the group."

> "hire an expert and only a factory can afford it"

> "The Thinking Processes, finally usable by groups without a facilitator"

Taken together, these say that experts were the bottleneck and AI removes them. "Finally usable" implies the method was unusable until you arrived, which is insulting to a community that has spent thirty years using it, teaching it to children and publishing it freely. "Leave the tree with the group" implies consultants walk off with it; good TP practice leaves the group owning the tree, because owning it is the buy-in. And "the same rigour" (see §3) is a claim you cannot support, put to the exact people best placed to check it.

The practitioner row is also the only one whose "one action" is "tell us where it is wrong". That is the right instinct, but it sits under a promise that we are no longer needed.

Reframe so practitioners are the multiplier, not the competition:

- **Promise to practitioners:** "Your method, between sessions. The skill keeps a group's trees current, attributed and open to scrutiny between the times you are in the room, so the work does not decay when you leave, and groups that could never book you can still start properly."
- **Competitive frame:** take "consultants" off the competitor list entirely. Replace it with something like: "Facilitators and TOC practitioners. The method's rigour comes from them. We make it cheaper to keep a tree alive between sessions and easier for a group to begin one; we do not replace the judgement of someone who has scrutinised a thousand arrows."
- **Lineage line:** credit TOCICO, TOCfE, Dettmer and Goldratt by name in the guide, and link to the practitioner community. That is how you get practitioners to send you groups.

## 3. Overclaim: what the skill does versus what the positioning implies

Checked against `skills/reason-commons/SKILL.md`.

| Positioning says | Skill actually does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "it adds the structure *after* people talk, by AI, so the conversation stays a conversation" | Live, turn-by-turn facilitation between one **driver** at a keyboard and the agent; it asks one focused question per turn and captures as it goes. It states that it is "Not for batch document extraction". | **Contradicted.** Structure is added *during* a conversation with the AI, not after a group's own conversation. "After" describes the retired forum-reading demo, which your §6 rightly calls aspirational. This is the biggest gap, and it is in the headline statement. |
| "turns ordinary discussion into a shared, addressable tree" | Turns a facilitated chat with an AI into YAML trees. A multi-person group only enters through speaker labels or a single driver. | Overstated. "Ordinary discussion" suggests ingesting a group's talk. |
| "AI drafts the trees from what a group already has, holds them consistent and does the updating" (narrative) | "If a prerequisite is missing, help establish it rather than filling it in yourself." Cross-tree links (`addresses`, `derived_from`, `implements`) "do not establish causal validity". No cross-tree consistency check exists; the validator checks schema and standing. | **Overclaim.** It holds state; it does not keep six trees logically consistent. To be fair, declining to fill gaps is the *right* TP behaviour, and you should brag about that instead. |
| "keeps the Categories of Legitimate Reservation as challenges rather than lectures" (§3) | The CLR are not named anywhere in the skill or its references. The "Test and accept" section asks plain-language versions of about five of them (clarity, causality, insufficiency, necessity vs helpfulness, predicted effect) and adds a good sufficiency/necessity warning. Additional cause, cause–effect reversal and tautology are not explicit, and nothing applies them systematically to each arrow. | **Partly true, overstated.** Either encode all eight in the skill or say "asks the method's standard questions of each connection". |
| "the same rigour" as consultants | No negative-branch reservation is required on FRT injections. No layers-of-resistance handling. It explicitly refuses to call something a constraint without evidence. | Overclaim, though the last point is genuinely to your credit. |
| "chooses the tree from what people say" | True: "Choose the tree that fits the question, without making users learn the terminology." | Fair. |
| 2R "tree of change ... drawn from the movement's written corpus" as proof for the skill | Produced by batch extraction (the `ltp-project` route), which `reason-commons` explicitly does not do. | Honest only if labelled as a different tool. |
| "records only what people endorse" (vs ChatGPT) | Records *everything* as `proposed`; only acceptance makes it `accepted`. | Wording error: it records proposals too. Say "treats nothing as accepted until a person accepts it". |

What it genuinely does well, and what the positioning undersells to me: attribution of the *idea* rather than the typist; explicit proposed / accepted / disputed standing, with the objector kept on the item; acceptance defined as "willing to build on, not proof"; refusing to manufacture a constraint; refusing to invent links; keeping AI suggestions separate from human contributions; and backlog work tracked on arrows as well as boxes. Those are exactly the habits a good facilitator tries to instil, and they are the honest pitch to me.

## 4. What would make me try it on a tree I already keep

1. **Import my existing tree.** Let me paste a CRT or cloud from Flying Logic, a Visio export or plain indented text, and have it land as accepted nodes attributed to me. Without that, "run the skill on a problem you have already treed" means retyping it. Is there an import path? The `ltp-project` / app route is "real but rougher"; tell me exactly which one to use.
2. **A scrutiny mode.** "Walk every arrow in this CRT and raise any CLR that applies, citing the category, and record it as a dispute." If it finds a real additional cause or reversal I missed, I am converted. If it flags noise, I will tell you, which is the feedback loop you want.
3. **Sufficiency and necessity kept straight.** Show me it will not let an AND junction collapse into ORs, and that it reads a cloud with necessity logic. The skill's "preserve joint causes" line is promising; demo it on a real cloud.
4. **Negative-branch reservation on injections.** When I add an injection to an FRT, does it ask what could go wrong? If not, it is not the TP.
5. **Between-session persistence for clients.** The real value to me is a client group continuing to add UDEs and evidence between my sessions, with standing and attribution preserved so I can review a diff. Show that workflow (snapshots plus latest-change diagram) and you have a practitioner tool.
6. **No marketing that undercuts me to my clients.** If your homepage tells my client they no longer need me, I will not recommend you to them.

## The 3 most important changes, ranked

1. **Fix the headline overclaim: "after people talk".** The positioning statement says structure is added *after* ordinary discussion. The shipped skill facilitates live, one driver with the AI, and explicitly does no batch extraction. Say "AI facilitates and files the tree as you talk, and nothing stands until a person accepts it". Keep "after" for the forum-reading aim, marked as next.
2. **Turn consultants from competitors into allies.** Drop "unlike ... consultants", "finally usable ... without a facilitator" and "only a factory can afford it". Position the skill as keeping a tree alive and scrutinised between facilitated sessions, and as an on-ramp for groups that cannot hire anyone, with practitioners credited and invited in.
3. **Get the method facts right and rest credibility on rigour, not storage.** Change "forty-year-old" to an accurate date (TP from the early 1990s; LTP 2007). Say five or six trees with the cloud correctly named, and stop selling "six trees in your head". Replace the cognitive-load story with the true difficulty (scrutinising every arrow, sufficiency vs necessity, buy-in), and then either encode all eight CLR in the skill or stop claiming them.
