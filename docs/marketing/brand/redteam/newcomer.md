# Red team: the sceptical newcomer

Who I am: I'm in a climate network. We argue on Discord, a forum and a lot of Zoom calls. I care about the mission and I've sat through about forty pitches telling me "AI will fix collaboration". I've never heard of LTP, issue trees, Goldratt or Dettmer. I give a site 20 seconds, and if it smells like a deck I close it.

## 1. My 20-second read

What I think this is, in my own words, after the mast plus the plain version: *"An AI thing that summarises our group's discussions into a mind-map, so we don't lose what we said. It's not an app. You install it into... some AI agent? Made by two people who use it on their own project."*

That is very close to "AI meeting notes with a diagram". The draft never deals with that reading, and it's the first one I'll reach for.

What I'd do next: nothing. The newcomer row in §3 gives me "Read one explainer, then look at a real tree." That's homework, not an action. The only real action anywhere is `npx skills add life-itself/reasoncommons -s reason-commons`, and I don't know what npx is, I don't have "an AI agent" and I'm not going to get Claude Code to try a mind-map.

The core mismatch: **the promise aimed at me is the part that doesn't exist yet.** §3 promises me "Your group's conversations stop evaporating". My group's conversations happen on Discord and a forum. §6 says reading a forum or Discord thread is "*Aspirational* … Demoed with rehearsal material only; the `contribution-proposals` skill that did it is retired." So my headline benefit is aspirational, and the evidence row in my own audience line admits it: "The forum-to-tree reading was shown only as a rehearsal with fictional contributions." If I find that out after clicking, and I will, the whole site turns into hype for me.

## 2. Words that lose me

Ranked roughly by how fast they lose me:

- **"skill"** / **"AI agent"** / **"`SKILL.md`-capable agents"**: in my world a "skill" is an Alexa thing or a line on a CV. "There is no app to sign up for. There is a skill you can install in your AI agent today" sounds like you're bragging about a missing feature. For me "no app" means "not for me".
- **"addressable"** ("a shared, addressable tree"): engineering talk. I can see what "every claim at an address" is getting at, but "addressable" on its own is a wall.
- **"compound"**: I read it as compound interest, so finance, crypto and growth-hacking. In a climate community "compounding" sits next to "returns", not next to "conversations".
- **"tree"**: half fine. "Mind-map" I'd get. "Tree of change" and "tree of action" are new nouns I have to learn. "Theory of change" I *already* know: every NGO and funder I deal with uses it. §1 #10 uses "a shared theory of change", which is the most familiar phrase in the whole draft for my world, and it's buried at the bottom of the table.
- **"accepted" / "standing" / "acceptance gate" / "proposed until a person explicitly accepts"**: sounds like bureaucracy. Who accepts? A vote? The admins? It reads like a moderation queue.
- **"annotations", "merge", "pull requests for discourse", "numbered snapshot"**: GitHub language. Fine for builders, but the tone section says "a little lightness ('pull requests for discourse') is allowed". For me it isn't lightness, it's a password I don't have.
- **"attributed"**: fine, though "who said what" is better and you already use it.
- **"critical success factors", "Categories of Legitimate Reservation", "Thinking Processes"**: consultant-speak. Okay in a guide, fatal anywhere I'll see it in the first minute.
- **"forty-year-old method"** with no name I recognise: "a method that works" asks me to take it on trust. Works for whom? Toyota-style factories, per the narrative. My network is not a factory.

The positioning statement in §2 is one 90-word sentence with two semicolons and a parenthesis. It's internal, but it's the source the copy will be cut from, and every clause in it is abstract.

## 3. Why I suspect hype, or an AI wrapper

- **The proof is the authors.** "We use it on our own collaboration" means two people. "Every pull request to the repo names the reasoning it serves" is proof that you like your own process, not that a group like mine gets anything from it. §6 says it plainly: "*Aspirational:* evidence that it changes outcomes for a group other than us." That one line is the whole trust problem.
- **The Second Renaissance tree doesn't prove the leap.** It was "drawn from the movement's written corpus", which means documents, not conversation. The headline is about conversation ("Conversations that compound"). The one real external tree shows a different, older thing: AI summarising documents into a diagram. ChatGPT already does that.
- **The ChatGPT rebuttal reads like a wrapper.** "The skill keeps state, attribution and an explicit acceptance gate." To me that's a prompt and a file. That's fine, and it might even be the selling point (open, portable, yours), but say it straight instead of dressing it up as a moat. If I find out it's a markdown prompt after being sold "a structure you can click on", I feel conned.
- **The "loop" story is a founder's story.** "Hard to hold → no one uses it → no one knows about it → never common practice … AI breaks the loop." Every AI pitch I've heard is shaped exactly like this: an old good thing was too expensive, AI makes it cheap. It's the most generic part of the narrative, and the calls rightly push it to second place. Keep it out of the first screen entirely.
- **Unsourced numbers.** "$2k a day" and "six trees held in your head" are flagged in §6 as Rufus's own figures. Any unsourced number in the first minute reads as hype. Cut them from anything newcomer-facing.
- **"that gets better every time it talks"**: unfalsifiable and unproven. Better by what measure?
- **Missing competitors.** The competitive frame covers Kialo, Polis, Loomio, Google Docs and consultants. It leaves out what I actually use or get pitched: **Otter, Fathom, Granola, Fireflies, Notion AI, Miro AI, Discord summary bots.** "AI reads our conversation and gives us something structured" is their pitch too. Without a sentence on why this isn't a meeting summariser, I'll file it as one.
- **"Unlike … consultants … it adds the structure *after* people talk, by AI"**: "by AI" at the end of a clause, with nothing about how, is the exact phrasing that makes me suspicious.

What partly works against my suspicion: the honesty rule in §5 ("Mark what is built, what is being built and what is the aim, in those words, every time") and "Not 'AI decides'". If the site really does label things that way, I'll trust it more than most. But the recommended mast breaks that rule, because it headlines the not-yet-true thing.

## 4. The taglines, ranked by "would I click?"

1. **"Thinking you can point at."** Best for me. It's concrete, a bit odd, and makes me want to see what it means. It doesn't promise transformation, so it doesn't set off my hype alarm. Weakness: abstract until I see the picture, so it only works with a real tree right beside it.
2. **"Talk normally. Keep everything you work out."** Directly answers my fear ("do I have to learn a tool?"). Plain. But "everything" is an overclaim, and it's the forum promise again, which isn't shipped.
3. **"Give your group's reasoning somewhere to live."** Warm and honest. "Reasoning" sounds like work, but "somewhere to live" is nice.
4. **"Collaboration that remembers."** Clear, but every meeting-notes AI could say it word for word.
5. **"Conversations that compound."** Sounds like a podcast title or a VC newsletter. "Compound" is finance. It's clever, and §5 says "Plain, not clever." It doesn't tell me what happens, and §1 rates it 2/3 on "true today" and admits "not yet true for a forum". For me that's disqualifying, because a forum is where my conversations happen.
6. **"Collaboration with a backbone."** Sounds like a management book. "Backbone" means discipline, and that's what my community is tired of.
7. **"Pull requests for discourse."** Means nothing to me.

The rest are forgettable.

**My proposal: "Stop having the same argument twice."**

It names a pain my community has and recognises at once (the research-circle story from the talk, and §3's own "disagreements get an address instead of being had again next year"). It's plain, a little funny, and true of what the skill does today (it keeps what was accepted, what's disputed and who said what). It doesn't say "AI" in the first breath. Line beneath it: *"Your group talks the way it already does. AI turns the discussion into a map of what you agree on, what you don't, and what you'll do, and nothing goes on the map until a person says so."*

Alternatives that keep the same strategy (lead with the pain, not the mechanism):

- "Where did that conversation go?" (the talk's own title, *Where Does Our Thinking Go?*, which is a better hook than any candidate in the table)
- "Your group's theory of change, built from its conversations." This one is aimed squarely at climate and movement people, using a word we already own.

## 5. What would actually convince me

In order of persuasive power:

1. **A before/after I can click within 5 seconds.** On the left, a real, messy thread (a real Discord or forum exchange, with names). On the right, the tree that came out of it, each node linked back to the message it came from, with accepted / disputed / open marked. If I can click a node and land on the exact sentence someone said, I believe you. That one image does more than every sentence in §2.
2. **A group other than you.** One named community, even a small one, that used it for a month, with a sentence from one of their members. "We'd argued about X for a year; this showed us we actually disagreed about Y." Until then, say plainly that you're looking for the first group, and invite me to be it. That's a stronger hook than pretending it's done.
3. **A path that doesn't need Claude Code.** "Paste a thread here, get a tree" (even as a hosted demo on fixed sample input), or at least a 60-second screen recording of a real session. If the honest answer is "right now you need Claude Code or Codex", say so in one line, and give me the *look at a tree* path as the main path rather than homework.
4. **The thing next to my existing tool.** A screenshot of the tree next to an Otter or ChatGPT summary of the same conversation, showing what the summary loses: who said what, what was disputed, what was agreed, what's still open.
5. **A failure or limit stated plainly.** "It's bad at X." Nothing builds trust with a sceptic faster.

What would *not* convince me: the 40-year lineage, Goldratt, the glossary entry count, the PR template, the self-model, the loop diagram. Those are proof for practitioners and builders. Keep them a click deeper.

## The 3 most important changes, ranked

1. **Lead with a real, clickable before/after (messy thread → tree, with every node linked to its source sentence), not a slogan.** Make it a real conversation, ideally from a group that isn't you. Until the forum-reading part ships, don't headline it: say "next" and show what the skill does today on a real session transcript.
2. **Replace "Conversations that compound" with a line that names the pain in plain words, e.g. "Stop having the same argument twice."** Keep "Thinking you can point at" as the line under the picture. Cut "addressable", "compound", "skill", "accepted standing", "pull request" and the $2k figure from anything a newcomer sees first. Use "theory of change" for movement audiences.
3. **Give newcomers a real action that needs no install, and admit the wrapper honestly.** Make "look at a real tree" a single click, not homework, and be explicit that running it today needs Claude Code or Codex. Add one sentence on why this isn't an AI meeting summariser (Otter, Granola, Discord bots), and say plainly that it's an open prompt and file format you own. Say "open", not "no app".
