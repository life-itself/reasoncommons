The analysis's ten tests sit on the connections they test, and each obstacle carries a question to check at a host before relying on it, because most of the first steps are the analysis's own planning.

**How credits were checked.** The step 1 rules apply unchanged: a forum credit names a post and that post's author, which the build checks; an essay credit cites the section the analysis points to; and the analysis's own reconstructions and inferences say so. Some statements sit in more than one view, as in the analysis's own sub-trees. Without the three connections imported as feedback, nothing leads back to itself. The third, mission drift reinforcing normalisation (R15), keeps the analysis's feedback label and is drawn as a loop, but no published chain leads from normalisation back to mission drift, so it closes no loop. **Not checked.** Leif Eriksson's five essays were not opened; essay credits rest on the analysis's section references and say so. None of the people credited has reviewed the paraphrases, and nothing is quoted.

**Site.** Every question except the goal is shown as titled parts: six for the causes, five conflicts, ten for the changes and risks, eight for the first steps and eight actions. Feedback returns round the right-hand side as a labelled loop and stays out of the layer ranking; a remedy's line ends in a bar at its risk. Each part lists where it leads beyond itself, including what each change is meant to address, the conflict it comes from and what each step and action puts into practice. The overview is built from reviewed blocks in the registry, including one path from a problem through its conflict and a change to the goal and a first action, every step of which the build checks against published connections and references. Actions show “Who: unassigned” with the proposed role. One banner says that everything is proposed, so cards and boxes carry credits instead of a badge, in this commons only. Across all three commons, the Statements tab now counts statements and connections separately, and the map page explains its colours.

**Publication checks.** Projection version 4 now also refuses a statement left out of every view, an overview path step that is not a published connection, chain of connections or cross-question reference, a published item recorded as retired, a removal of anything not retired and a proposed role on anything but an action. All 63 publication, meaning, topology and layout tests pass, ten of them new: loops round the side, remedy bars, joint sources drawn together in every part, loops closing only through feedback, the overview path, the retired connection, the owner slot, the single banner, and the tab counts and colour key across all commons. ### Reading boundary

The commons is a compact proposed AI reconstruction from two user-supplied analyses, not a transcript or participant-authored record. It contains exactly the approved 22 proposed statements and 15 proposed connections across six trees. Every statement and connection has a locator into a supplied analysis; inferred relationships are labelled. The original forum posts, five underlying documents and historical claims were not independently verified. The suggested introductory forum post in the supplied text was not sent.

The supplied PDF is *Second Renaissance Causal and Prerequisite Trees*, version 2, 22 October 2026, 48 pages. Its SHA-256 is `0f9e845ba9eca0c3b903e92084d9633f756c216696c38458f6e1f9bd0de3a0df`. Source links in item views lead to those descriptions, with page, section and proposition references; they do not pretend to be verified direct links to unavailable primary passages.

### What the first view says

The overview opens with proposed common ground, then the capability/autonomy conflict, a possible conflict-guidance comparison, benefit and harm together, and one unassigned first action. It retains the wider programme as source notes and review backlog: reflective development, the Laboratory, Forge/model adaptation, Vocal Presence, federation, material services and security. Conflict guidance is one entry point. No measured global constraint is claimed, and the spiritual-root account remains a philosophical hypothesis.

The initial hypotheses do not establish that AI is essential, that a local gain changes institutional selection, or that satisfaction proves just treatment. Open work includes optional-AI and human comparisons, competence without the tool, suppressed grievances, the relative gain for small democratic groups, and defining progress through viable practices retained without mission drift. No host, owner, budget, baseline, numerical target, trial date, feedback commitment or observed outcome is assigned. Local obstacles must be checked rather than inferred from missing document details.
